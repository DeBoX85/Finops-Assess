# Cloud cost check-in: questions for your leadership team
About 10 minutes. No technical knowledge needed. There are no right or wrong answers. Pick the statement that is closest to today. "Not sure" is a fine answer and tells us where to look.
Who should answer: whoever knows most about how cloud spending is managed (often the CIO, CFO, or head of IT). You can split it between people.

Name and role: ____________   Company: ____________   Date: ____________
Roughly how many people use or build on Azure? ____________

For each question choose A, B, C or D (D = Not sure).

1. Who is responsible for cloud costs?
   A. No one in particular. IT deals with it when something looks wrong.
   B. A named person or team, but they mostly react to problems.
   C. A named owner and a regular review (at least monthly) that includes finance and IT.

2. Do leaders set goals for cloud spending?
   A. No specific goals.
   B. There is a budget or a rough target, reviewed occasionally.
   C. Clear targets tied to business goals, reviewed regularly by leadership.

3. Before a new system is built, is its cloud cost estimated?
   A. Rarely. We find out afterwards.
   B. Sometimes, for larger projects.
   C. Always, and we compare the estimate with the real bill later.

4. How do you predict next quarter's cloud bill?
   A. We don't, or we assume it stays the same.
   B. Someone estimates it from last month's bill.
   C. A regular forecast that accounts for planned changes, checked against actuals.

5. Can each team see what it costs and who pays?
   A. No. The bill comes as one total.
   B. Partly. Some costs are split by team.
   C. Yes. Costs are assigned to teams or products and shown or charged back.

6. If the bill suddenly jumps, what happens?
   A. We usually notice on the invoice, weeks later.
   B. Someone notices and investigates, but there is no set process.
   C. We get an alert quickly and there is a known process to find the cause and fix it.

7. Do you know what the cloud costs per customer, product or transaction?
   A. No.
   B. Roughly, for one or two key products.
   C. Yes, tracked regularly and used in decisions.

8. When unused or oversized cloud resources are found, what happens?
   A. Nothing systematic.
   B. Someone cleans up now and then.
   C. There is an owner and a routine (for example monthly), and results are tracked.

9. Who decides on discounts and long-term commitments (like reservations or savings plans)?
   A. No one has looked at them.
   B. Decided case by case, without a regular review.
   C. A named owner reviews commitments regularly against usage.

10. Are there rules to keep spending in check (for example required labels or allowed sizes)?
    A. No written rules.
    B. Some rules, applied inconsistently.
    C. Agreed rules that are enforced, with a process for exceptions.

11. Do the people who build and approve cloud services understand cloud cost basics?
    A. Not really.
    B. Some do, there is little training.
    C. Yes, there is training or onboarding and cost is part of how teams work.

12. Do IT, finance, security, procurement and sustainability teams work together on cost?
    A. Mostly separately.
    B. They talk when needed.
    C. Regular joint meetings or shared goals.

13. How do people get cloud cost reports, and how do you check how well you are doing?
    A. Mostly the invoice, with no regular review of our approach.
    B. Reports from the portal or spreadsheets, and we occasionally review how we manage cost.
    C. Automated reports for each audience, and we review our approach on a schedule.

Anything else we should know (recent changes, upcoming projects, concerns)?
